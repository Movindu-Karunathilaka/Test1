Garage Full Throttle · customer app · design proposal 2

Appointments refresh

The same treatment as the Home refresh, applied to the Appointment tab. Today it's a search box, a row of status filters and an empty list. The new version follows each job from booking to pick-up, lets customers approve extra work without a phone call, and keeps every past service as a digital logbook. Everything shown is example data.

Goal: fewer "is my car ready?" calls Goal: faster yes on extra work Goal: more repeat bookings

  1. 1

    Same header as Home Small

    Replaces: bell + profile only, no points

    Points chip, wordmark, notifications and profile, the same on every tab. The refresh button goes; pull down to refresh instead.

  2. 2

    A real Book button Small

    Replaces: small "+ Add" outline button

    Booking is the reason this tab exists, so it gets the red primary button next to the title. "Add" also sounded like adding a record; "Book" says what happens.

  3. 3

    Upcoming / History switch Small

    Replaces: five status chips that run off the screen

    Most customers have one or two active jobs, so filtering by status adds nothing. Status moves onto each card. Search and filters move into History, where the list actually gets long.

  4. 4

    Live job card with times Medium

    Upgrade of: the status tracker on Home

    The job in the workshop sits at the top with a timeline: when it was booked, checked in and started, plus the expected ready time. Each step sends a push notification (see section 02).

  5. 5

    Workshop photos Medium

    GFT addition

    The mechanic snaps worn parts from the admin side: brake pads, tyre tread, a dirty filter. Customers see exactly what the problem is, which builds trust and makes the next step an easy yes.

  6. 6

    Approve extra work in the app Large

    GFT addition

    Instead of phone tag mid-job, the customer sees the part, the price and a clear choice: Approve or Call me first. Approval is timestamped on the job for the garage's records. Tap Approve in the mockup.

  7. 7

    Upcoming booking card Small

    Replaces: a plain list row

    A calendar date tile, the service, the car and the time, with three one-tap actions: add to the phone's calendar, reschedule, and directions to the garage.

  8. 8

    Due-soon suggestion Medium

    Links to: the service meter on Home

    When a car on the account is close to its next service, a dashed card suggests booking it. One tap opens the booking sheet with the car and service already filled in.

  9. 9

    History as a digital logbook Medium

    Replaces: search across all appointments

    Past jobs grouped by year with the date, car, odometer, amount and points earned, plus a yearly total. Each has its invoice and a Book again button. A full service history also helps when the customer sells the car.

  10. 10

    Ready to pick up, and pay Large

    Replaces: a red "Ready To P…" chip

    It turns green so it reads as good news. It shows the itemised bill, pick-up deadline and points to be earned. Paying in the app (Apple Pay / card) means a faster handover at the counter. Without payment, it still works as a bill preview.

  11. 11

    First-booking empty state Small

    Replaces: "No appointments for now"

    An illustration, a reward for the first booking and the garage's most popular services as shortcuts, including Roadworthy (RWC) for Victorian buyers and sellers.

04 · What changes from today

Five problems with the current tab

Status chips run off the screen"Ready To P…" is cut off, and nothing shows the row scrolls. Each chip is also outlined in its own colour, so they all look selected. Replaced by the Upcoming / History switch (3).
The main action is the smallest button"+ Add" is a small outlined button in the top corner, the hardest place to reach with one thumb. Booking becomes the red primary button (2), and the Book button on Home opens the same sheet.
Two ways to refreshThere's a refresh button on the tab and another on Home. Pull to refresh is the standard on iOS and Android, and frees up the space.
In progress and Ready look the sameBoth are red. Ready becomes green (10).
The empty screen is a dead end"No appointments for now" gives a new customer nothing to do. It becomes a first-booking prompt with popular services (11).

05 · Build order

Ship it in three drops

These can run alongside the Home phases. Phase 1 here is mostly front-end work on data the app already stores.

Phase 1 · Clean-up

About 1–2 weeks. Existing data.

  • Shared header, Book button (1, 2)
  • Upcoming / History switch (3)
  • New booking cards + calendar and directions (7)
  • Green "Ready" status, pull to refresh
  • First-booking empty state (11)

Needs: a timestamp saved on each status change.

Phase 2 · Live jobs

About 3 weeks. Admin + push.

  • Live job timeline (4)
  • Push notification per status
  • 3-step booking sheet with free slots
  • History logbook with invoices (9)
  • Due-soon suggestion (8)

Needs: push notifications; workshop slot capacity per day; invoice PDF upload; odometer recorded at each job.

Phase 3 · In-app approvals

About 4 weeks. New features.

  • Workshop photos from the admin side (5)
  • Approve extra work in the app (6)
  • Itemised bill and in-app payment (10)

Needs: photo upload in the admin; quote line items with approve / call-me responses; a payment provider such as Stripe or Square (Apple Pay and Google Pay).
